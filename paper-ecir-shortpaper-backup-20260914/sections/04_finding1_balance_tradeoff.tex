We report each dataset's primary retrieval direction as Dir.\ A -- text$\to$image (T$\to$I) for MSCOCO and VisualNews, composed (image,text)$\to$image for FashionIQ -- and, where the dataset is bidirectional, image$\to$text (I$\to$T) as Dir.\ B. Table~\ref{tab:main} shows no consistent effect of balance regularization on Dir.\ A Recall across datasets. On MSCOCO, strong regularization is significantly better than vanilla: $23.33\pm0.11\%$ vs.\ $20.78\pm0.06\%$ ($n{=}5$ seeds each), non-overlapping $\pm1$-std bands, $p<10^{-6}$ (Welch's $t$-test), a $+12.3\%$ relative gain -- and the gain holds across 3 independently retrained RQ tokenizers, not just decoder seeds: vanilla mean $21.16\pm0.47\%$ vs.\ strong mean $24.32\pm0.86\%$, every strong tokenizer beating every vanilla tokenizer. (Absolute values here are well below published GENIUS numbers -- see Section~\ref{sec:setup} -- reflecting this study's matched, from-scratch pipeline rather than a state-of-the-art reproduction.) On VisualNews, the same strong regularization instead \emph{halves} Recall ($20.07\%\to9.43\%$, $-53\%$ relative; $n{=}3$ seeds each, Welch's $t$-test, $p<10^{-7}$) -- a reversal of sign on a second broad-domain, bidirectional corpus. On FashionIQ, vanilla wins outright at every regularized $\lambda$; the ranking \emph{among} regularized strengths is not seed-stable (all values sub-1\% R@1), so we report only that any regularization hurts, not a specific optimal $\lambda$.

This gain is also direction-specific -- the same MSCOCO checkpoints that improve on Dir.\ A collapse on Dir.\ B (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Section~\ref{sec:finding2}) -- and its sign even reverses on a second broad-domain, bidirectional dataset (Section~\ref{sec:finding3}); we examine both next.
